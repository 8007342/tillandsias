      arm      ExecMainStart -> "vsock listener bound"
      BEFORE   81.286 ms  /  74.376 ms
      AFTER     5.868 ms  /   5.802 ms

  ~13× faster, ~72 ms removed. AFTER varies by 66 µs across samples; BEFORE by

- **The shape is the proof**: BEFORE, the vsock spawn is *issued* at 9.3 ms but
  the listener does not *bind* until 81.3 ms — 72 ms sitting in the injection
  queue behind five spawns on one worker. AFTER, spawn-issue and bind carry the
  **same** journal timestamp. That is the packet's mechanism, observed.

- **I had to make it observable first, and that is the finding worth carrying.**
  The bind left NO trace: the `info!` after `bind()` has no subscriber in the
  guest — verified by searching the whole guest filesystem for `tillandsias.log`
  (absent) and grepping the unit's journal (no such line). Criterion 3 was
  **unsatisfiable by anyone** as the code stood.
- **My first attempt measured the wrong quantity and nearly became a false
  negative.** Using the vsock *preflight* line as a proxy for the spawn gave
  BEFORE 6.624 ms vs AFTER 6.605 ms — a 19 µs difference — and I was one step
  from recording "the reordering does not help". `tokio::spawn` only enqueues,
  so issuing five spawns costs microseconds; the proxy timed the *issue*, not the
  *bind*. A near-null result from a proxy is exactly the shape that gets
  believed, because it reads as a careful negative.
- Fixed with one `eprintln!` at the instant `bind()` returns — stderr from this
  unit demonstrably reaches the journal with a monotonic stamp (the preflight
  line proves the path). It is now a permanent observable for anyone
  re-measuring this seam.
